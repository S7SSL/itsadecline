NatWest Declined My Mortgage — What To Do Next

NatWest is one of the UK's high-volume mainstream lenders — which means a large share of borrowers pass through their criteria each year, and a meaningful share get declined. Their automated decisioning mirrors the other mainstream banks: clean credit file, two-year income history, standard property, evidenced deposit. Miss any of those and the application is usually rejected.

Common reasons NatWest declines mortgage applications

  • Adverse credit markers — CCJs, defaults, missed payments — in the last 24-36 months.
  • Self-employed income that does not fit two years of accounts or SA302s.
  • Limited company directors where salary + dividends is below the required multiple.
  • Affordability that fails NatWest's stress test at current rates.
  • Property type flagged non-standard (concrete, timber frame, deck-access flats, short lease).
  • Deposit source that cannot be evidenced cleanly against anti-money-laundering rules.

Why RBS, Coutts and other mainstream banks are not the answer

RBS (Royal Bank of Scotland) is in the same banking group as NatWest and shares most of the criteria. A NatWest decline is usually an RBS decline. Coutts operates a different underwriting model but requires very high income and asset levels typical of the private-banking client base. For the large majority of borrowers, the right next step is not another mainstream brand but a specialist lender with different criteria.

What specialist lenders do

Specialist UK lenders are all FCA-authorised, same as NatWest. The difference is their product range and their willingness to underwrite cases case-by-case. They price the additional risk into the rate rather than rejecting outright.

Specialist products that commonly replace a declined NatWest application:

  • Adverse-credit mortgages for cases with CCJs, defaults or missed payments.
  • Self-employed mortgages accepting latest-year accounts, retained profits or contract income.
  • CCJ-specific products for cases with one or more county court judgments.
  • Bridging finance for property-driven declines.

Your next step

  1. Do not fire another mainstream application. Hard searches accumulate fast.
  2. Pull your credit file from all three bureaux to see what NatWest saw.
  3. Identify the category of decline — credit, affordability, property or policy.
  4. Match the case to a specialist lender whose criteria specifically accepts that category. A specialist broker or lead service (like ours) can do this efficiently.

Frequently asked questions

Is NatWest stricter than other mainstream banks?
NatWest's criteria are broadly comparable to other mainstream UK banks in the Lloyds, Nationwide and Barclays tier. Differences show up at the margin — affordability stress-testing, acceptable income sources, property type — rather than as categorical strictness.
Can I try RBS or Coutts after NatWest declines?
RBS is part of the same NatWest Group and shares much of the same criteria; a NatWest decline usually means an RBS decline. Coutts is the private-bank arm with different underwriting but very high income and asset thresholds. Most applicants need a specialist lender, not a sister brand.
How long does a NatWest credit search stay on my file?
Around 12 months. It is visible to every subsequent lender during that period, though it weighs less the older it gets.
Are specialist lenders regulated in the same way as NatWest?
Yes. Every lender on our panel is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, same as NatWest. The difference is in their product range and their appetite for cases that mainstream banks reject.
